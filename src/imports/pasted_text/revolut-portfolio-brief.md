Case Study 1 Brief: GuardianOnboard
Project title
GuardianOnboard - Designing a clearer onboarding and KYC experience for a digital finance product

1. Overview
Write a short paragraph:

GuardianOnboard is a self-initiated fintech product case study focused on improving the onboarding and identity verification experience for new users. The goal was to reduce friction, improve trust, and make the KYC process feel more transparent and manageable.

2. Problem
Users often drop off during onboarding when identity verification feels slow, unclear, or intrusive. In digital finance products, poor KYC communication can increase frustration, reduce activation, and create support issues.

3. My role
Product Designer
Owned the project end-to-end
Conducted research
Designed flows, wireframes, and UI
Created prototype
Ran user testing
Iterated based on findings
4. Users
Define target users such as:

first-time digital finance users
users unfamiliar with KYC
users who are worried about privacy and trust
users who want fast setup with clear progress
5. Research
Add 3-4 research findings like:

users felt anxious when they did not know why personal documents were needed
users wanted visibility into how many steps remained
unclear error states caused frustration
users needed more reassurance around privacy and data use
6. Product goals
reduce onboarding friction
improve clarity in the KYC journey
increase completion and activation
reduce support queries
7. Design process
Explain:

mapped current onboarding journey
identified friction points
simplified step-by-step progression
introduced progress tracking and clearer microcopy
improved information hierarchy
designed reassurance moments around privacy and verification
8. Wireframes / flows
Show:

onboarding journey map
low-fidelity wireframes
step-by-step KYC flow
decision points
error and success states
9. UI design
Mention:

accessible layout
clear visual hierarchy
reduced cognitive load
trust-building UI patterns
mobile-first design decisions
10. User testing
This is very important. Add a section like this:

User testing approach
I conducted usability testing with 5 users to evaluate whether the onboarding flow felt clear, trustworthy, and easy to complete.

Testing goals
understand whether users could complete onboarding without confusion
test whether progress tracking reduced anxiety
identify unclear microcopy or verification steps
test trust and clarity in privacy-related messaging
Key findings
users responded positively to visible progress indicators
privacy reassurance screens improved confidence
some users still hesitated at document upload
clearer guidance on acceptable document types reduced friction
Changes made after testing
simplified upload instructions
improved error messaging
refined progress tracker labels
added clearer privacy reassurance copy
11. Iteration
Show before/after examples. Explain what changed and why.

12. Final solution
Summarise the final onboarding solution:

guided step flow
trust-focused UI
improved copy
transparent verification status
clearer upload steps
13. Success metrics
Use this exact framing:

Defined success metrics

Activation uplift: 12-20%
Onboarding time reduction: 20-30%
Reduction in KYC-related support issues: 15-25%
14. What I learned
Example: This project showed me how trust, transparency, and microcopy can significantly shape the onboarding experience in fintech products. It also reinforced the importance of testing flows with users before finalising high-stakes journeys.

Case Study 2 Brief: QuickPay
Project title
QuickPay - Improving clarity and confidence in cross-border payments

1. Overview
QuickPay is a self-initiated product case study focused on designing a clearer cross-border payments experience. The aim was to reduce uncertainty around fees, exchange rates, and transfer progress while improving trust and completion.

2. Problem
Users often hesitate during international transfers because fees, exchange rates, and status updates are not always presented clearly. This creates confusion, lowers trust, and can lead to drop-off.

3. My role
Product Designer
Owned research, product flow design, UI, prototype, testing, and iteration
4. Users
users sending money internationally
users sensitive to hidden fees
users who want reassurance that their transfer is progressing correctly
5. Research findings
users wanted fee transparency earlier in the journey
people felt anxious when exchange rates changed without warning
users wanted stronger progress and status feedback
confirmation screens were often too vague
6. Product goals
improve transfer clarity
reduce payment abandonment
improve trust in the experience
make status updates more transparent
7. Design process
mapped the existing payment journey
identified uncertainty points
redesigned rate preview and transfer summary
improved fee transparency and status tracking
simplified the confirmation experience
8. Wireframes / flows
Show:

transfer flow map
rate preview screens
fee breakdown screens
status tracking states
confirmation journey
9. UI design
Mention:

clean information hierarchy
strong emphasis on amounts, rates, and timings
trust indicators
accessible layout and readable typography
10. User testing
Add this:

User testing approach
I tested the payment flow with 5 users to understand how clearly they could interpret rates, fees, and progress updates.

Testing goals
assess whether users understood the full cost before confirming
identify confusion around exchange rates
evaluate whether transfer status updates felt reassuring
check whether confirmation screens built confidence
Key findings
early fee visibility improved confidence
clearer exchange rate explanations reduced hesitation
users preferred stronger transfer status messaging
improved hierarchy helped users scan financial information faster
Changes made after testing
moved fee breakdown earlier
simplified exchange rate language
strengthened confirmation copy
improved transfer status visuals
11. Iteration
Show what changed after testing and why.

12. Final solution
Summarise the final transfer flow and how it supports trust, transparency, and clarity.

13. Success metrics
Defined success metrics

Transfer completion uplift: 5-15%
Time-to-complete reduction: 10-25%
Reduction in post-transfer queries: 10-20%
14. What I learned
Example: This project strengthened my understanding of how financial clarity and confidence affect user behaviour. In payments, even small decisions in hierarchy and wording can influence trust and completion.

Case Study 3 Brief: FinSight Design System
Project title
FinSight - Building a scalable design system for fintech product experiences

1. Overview
FinSight is a design system case study created to support consistent onboarding, payments, and account management experiences across a fintech product ecosystem.

2. Problem
As products scale, inconsistency in UI patterns slows delivery, weakens usability, and creates fragmented user experiences. Fintech products especially need consistency because clarity and trust are critical.

3. My role
Product Designer
Built system structure, components, patterns, and accessibility guidance
4. Product goals
improve consistency across product areas
speed up handoff and delivery
support accessibility
make patterns scalable for future teams and features
5. Process
audited interface patterns
identified repeated components
defined tokens, typography, colours, spacing, and reusable elements
documented states and behaviours
structured handoff logic for future scalability
6. Components
Show:

buttons
text fields
input states
date picker
steppers
cards
banners
status indicators
7. Accessibility
This is very important for Revolut. Include:

contrast considerations
clear focus states
hierarchy for readability
scalable typography
consistency of interaction states
8. User testing / validation
Even design systems can include testing. Add:

Validation approach
I tested key component patterns with users and reviewed them against accessibility and usability principles to ensure they were intuitive, consistent, and scalable.

What I checked
whether key actions were easy to identify
whether forms and buttons felt predictable
whether the visual hierarchy supported readability
whether interaction states were clear
Improvements made
refined spacing for readability
strengthened focus states
simplified component labels
improved consistency across form behaviour
9. Handoff and governance
Include this because it makes you sound stronger:

reusable tokens
component naming conventions
documented usage guidance
scalable patterns for future collaboration with developers
10. Success metrics
Defined success metrics

Design-to-development handoff time reduction: 20-30%
UI consistency improvement: 10-20%
11. What I learned
Example: This case study helped me understand how system thinking improves both user experience and team efficiency. In fintech, consistent patterns can reduce friction and help users feel more confident in complex tasks.
